## What the real material says

Every number below was measured on 2026-09-13 and 2026-09-15 by reading the
six captures through [`CaptureReader`](../src/zpfwire/capture.py) and
converting them with the tree as it stands. Scripts were throwaway; the
assertions become tests in the phases.

### The six captures

| File | Packets | Captured at | Repeats | TSval of the repeat vs. its original | Peer packets between original and repeat |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| `tcp_dup_ts.pcap` | 154 | the router (mirror) | 10 | **same**, 10 of 10 | **0** of 10 |
| `tcp_lossy_ts.pcap` | 234 | the sender | 16 | **later**, 16 of 16, by 40–101 ticks | ≥ 1 |
| `tcp_gap_ts.pcap` | 215 | the receiver | 0 — the original is absent; 13 fills | fill newer than everything committed past the hole: 9 strictly, 4 tied within the sender's own tick | — |
| `tcp_reorder_ts.pcap` | 267 | the receiver | 10 spurious resends | 8 delayed originals older than all 7–27 segments that passed them; the 10 resends later | **10 of 10** already acked by the peer when the resend arrived |
| `tcp_corrupt_ts.pcap` | 196 | the receiver | 5 resends of corrupted originals | **later**, 5 of 5, by 40–82 ticks; **content differs** in every pair; the first covering ACK echoes the *resend's* TSval, 5 of 5 | ≥ 1 |
| `tcp_wrap_ts.pcap` | 196 | the receiver | 4 spurious resends | server ISN `4294931070`, 36 226 bytes below 2³²; 56 post-wrap segments; 4 pre-wrap originals arrive after post-wrap data with an **older** TSval; the 4 resends later | 4 of 4 acked |

**Capture-time distance is the second signal.** A mirror copy lands **0.4–1.8
µs** after its original, TSval Δ = 0, with nothing from the peer in between.
A real resend lands **≥ 40 ms** later — at least the path's round trip — with
TSval Δ ≥ 40 and, wherever the reverse direction is captured, the peer's
duplicate ACKs between the two. Nothing in either population is within two
orders of magnitude of the other.

Every packet in five of the six files carries a **bad transport checksum**
(veth offload at the capturing host). Under the default `checksum="ignore"`
that costs nothing; under `"drop"` every byte would go. It is the practical
case for #18's evidence being independent of the checksum policy.
`tcp_corrupt_ts` is the exception, collected with offload off: its **5**
failing checksums are the corrupted copies and nothing else — the first
fixture where `bad_checksum` counts something that means what it says.

### What the tree writes for them today

| Capture | Defaults | `reorder_window=0` | Reads back clean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tcp_dup_ts` | 7 records, **3 flagged `retransmit`**, 10 regions `overlap-discarded`, 5 092 bytes | same | yes |
| `tcp_lossy_ts` | 13 records, 7 flagged, 16 regions, 8 384 bytes | same — from a sender's seat nothing was missing | yes |
| `tcp_gap_ts` | 10 records, 0 flagged, **0 gaps** — the default buffer absorbs every hole | 21 records, **11 gaps, 6 812 late bytes** | yes |
| `tcp_reorder_ts` | 9 records, **4 flagged**, 5 240 overlap | 17 records, **8 gaps, 9 432 late bytes** = 8 delayed originals (4 192) + 10 spurious resends (5 240) | yes |
| `tcp_corrupt_ts` | 8 records, 2 flagged, 2 620 overlap, `bad_checksum` 5 — **the 5 corrupt copies are written**; under `checksum="drop"` the clean resends are, and the two payloads differ at exactly 5 byte offsets | — | yes |
| `tcp_wrap_ts` | 7 records, 1 flagged, 2 096 overlap; `unplaceable == []` — the modular offset space places every post-wrap record | 8 records, 1 gap, 4 192 late bytes | yes, both |

Four of those cells are what the release changes. The dup file's 3 flags
overstate what the sender did — spec `0.20` says so in words now. The two
late-byte totals each bundle two causes that the option separates. The
corrupt file writes bytes the receiver never accepted, and the option says
which copy it did. And the reorder file's 4 flags are *right*: a spurious
fast retransmit is a real resend, later TSval, and stays flagged under
`0.20`. That cell is the regression test for not over-correcting.

The wrap file is the one that changes nothing and proves something: the
tree already carries a real stack's numbering through 2³² and zpf places
every record — `_advance`, `_delta` and the modular `seq_start − (isn + 1)`
all hold. Its four delayed pre-wrap originals are the case where a naïve
unsigned comparison would call the older TSval right and the sequence number
wrong; serial arithmetic already agrees with the TSval, and Phase 3's PAWS
must keep agreeing.

### The coarse-tick objection, answered

The `0.2.0` plan's reason for holding (1) was that a stack with a ~100 ms
timestamp tick would put a LAN fast retransmit inside the same tick as its
original, and a rule of *same TSval ⇒ duplicate* would clear the flag on a
real resend. packeteer's manifest confirms the shape is real even on Linux:
a veth pair with no `netem delay` puts a whole transfer inside one 1 ms tick,
and "a fast retransmit carrying the *same* TSval as its original" is why the
lossy pair was collected with 20 ms of delay.

The answer is not a threshold on capture time — there is no number that is
not a guess — but the signal that is *absent* from a mirror copy and
*present* in a fast retransmit: **a fast retransmit is triggered by the
peer's duplicate ACKs, and they sit in the capture between the original and
the resend**; a mirror copy follows its original with nothing in between.
Phase 1 states the rule; the limit it leaves — a one-way capture of a
coarse-tick stack — is documented as a limit, and the spec's own SHOULD
covers the tie.

### Why #18 is ours to hold, sequenced or not

#18 asked whether `favor-acked` might be offered only under `--sequenced`,
where "everything is already buffered". Checked: `--sequenced` opens the
session with `linearize=True`, and zpf buffers **built `Record` blocks**
([`writer.py:777`](../../python-zipline/src/zpf/writer.py#L777),
[`:923`](../../python-zipline/src/zpf/writer.py#L923)) — bytes already
committed to a payload, not amendable by design. That buffer is no help: a
copy cannot be swapped into a block that has been built. So the hold has to
be on **runs**, in [`TcpFlow`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py), before
`session.record()` — and that makes it independent of `--sequenced`, which
settles the question the issue left open. Both directions of a connection
already arrive at the same `TcpFlow`, so the peer's acks are in hand without
a new seam.

    That is the receiver's own rule (`SEG.SEQ <= Last.ACK.sent`), and it is
    load-bearing for Phase 3: while a hole is open, `TS.Recent` stays at the
    last in-order segment, which is why a delayed original with an older

  rule below degrades through the same door.

### The rule

A repeat — bytes for a range this direction already accepted or holds — is a
**duplicate** when *all* of:

1. both it and the transmission it repeats carried the option;
2. its TSval **equals** that transmission's;
3. **no packet from the peer** arrived between the two (`peer_count`
   unchanged).

Otherwise it is a **retransmission**, which is today's rule and spec
`0.20`'s SHOULD. The three failure modes are counted separately so the
fallback is never silent: `ts_absent` (no option on one or both), `ts_stale`
(the original has left `recent`), and — not a fallback — `ts_later` (a real
resend). Measured: 10 of 10 repeats in `tcp_dup_ts` satisfy all three
clauses; 0 of 36 resends across the other three files satisfy clause 3, and
0 satisfy clause 2 either.

Content is **not** compared. A resend carries the same bytes as its original
unless one was corrupted in flight, and corruption is the checksum policy's
business, not this rule's; comparing bytes would add nothing to the
distinction and would need the written bytes retained.

### The four sites

Every `_mark_retransmit` call is one decision point, and each gets the rule
in place of the assumption:

- [x] [`_accept:280`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py#L280) — wholly-repeated
  segment, via `_count_repeat`.
- [x] [`_accept:289`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py#L289) — leading trim.
- [x] [`_hold:332`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py#L332) — a repeat of a held
  segment; compare against `_Pending.tsval`.
- [x] [`_drain:358`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py#L358) — trim while draining.

A duplicate is **counted as `duplicate_bytes`, cited as
`REASON_DUPLICATE = "duplicate-discarded"`, and does not set
`retransmit`.** A retransmission is counted and cited as today.

  `_mark_retransmit` only ever marked an *open* run.  That is the same

  it has emitted — and it is not this phase's to change.  The test asserts

## Phase 3 — (4): PAWS and the sequence wrap — **DONE** (2026-09-16)

Two rules, both from RFC 7323, both bytes-moving. The real captures' role
here is to prove that **neither rule fires on any of the six** — a stronger
statement than "the rule is right": on a receiver-side capture of a real
stack, a rule that emulates the receiver must agree with what the receiver
did, and D-SACK in `tcp_reorder_ts` is the receiver saying it kept every
delayed original. `tcp_wrap_ts` adds the case that matters most for PAWS:
four pre-wrap originals with an older TSval arriving after post-wrap data
that the receiver had already acked — and the receiver kept them (their
resends are the spurious ones). A PAWS that rejected them would disagree
with the stack it emulates.

What `tcp_wrap_ts` does **not** hold is the thin zone the wrap rule is for.
Its delayed originals sit 1.5–3 KB behind `next_seq`, squarely inside serial
arithmetic's range, so `_delta` already places them; a segment that lands
just under 2³¹ behind is a shape no real capture has and the hand-built
frames remain the only positive test of that rule.

- [x] **PAWS.** In `_accept`, before anything else: a segment carrying the
  option whose TSval is **older** than `ts_recent` (signed 32-bit compare,
  RFC 7323 §5.3) is discarded — cited `REASON_PAWS = "paws-rejected"`,
  counted `paws_bytes`, no flag. Because `ts_recent` advances only on
  in-order arrivals (Phase 1), a delayed original behind an open hole is
  *not* older than `ts_recent` and passes, as it does on a real receiver.
  The rule also does not apply to the SYN.
- [x] **The wrap.** [`_delta`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py#L64) declares
  anything ≥ 2³¹ away as behind; the thin zone is a segment that lands in
  `(−2³¹, −2³¹ + reorder_window]` of `next_seq` — far enough behind that no
  retransmission could be that old, close enough to the half-space edge
  that a wrap would put it there. Such a segment with a TSval **newer than
  `max_tsval`** is from after the wrap: treat it as ahead and hold it.
  With an older TSval or no option, it is behind, as today. Counted
  `wrap_resolved` so a file that needed the rule says so.
- [x] Neither rule touches `ReassemblyOptions`.

### What Phase 3 execution changed

- **"Treat it as ahead and hold it" is not representable — twice over.**
  The pending buffer, `_next_pending` and `_drain` all compare with
  `_delta`, so a segment 2³¹ ahead cannot be *held*: the next drain calls
  it behind and discards it.  And zpf's writer refuses the record outright
  — records of one participant are stored in serial order, and a record
  more than 2³¹ past its predecessor with nothing between *precedes* it
  (`SemanticError: seq_start … precedes the participant's previous
  record`).  That is the format's rule, so what the format can say is that
  one session ended and another began.  The rule became **a session
  split**: `FlowTracker.splits_before(pkt)`, consulted by the conversion
  loop beside `expired()`; `TcpFlow` answers yes for a segment further
  behind than the largest window TCP can advertise whose TSval is newer
  than `ts_recent`, the session ends for a new reason **`capture-gap`**,
  and the segment opens the next — the same mechanism as the idle timeout.
- **The zone's lower edge is RFC 7323's maximum window (2³⁰), not
  `reorder_window`.**  The plan's `(−2³¹, −2³¹ + reorder_window]` was a
  guess at "further behind than any resend could be"; the RFC gives the
  bound (a 16-bit window scaled by at most 14), and it makes the rule
  cover a hole of any width above 2³¹ rather than a 256 KB sliver just past
  the edge.  A hole between 2³⁰ and 2³¹ needs no rule: serial arithmetic
  already calls it ahead.
- **PAWS is applied only to segments that would contribute bytes**, after
  the wholly-behind branch.  A repeat is judged as a repeat whatever its
  clock, because the flag a repeat may set is the sender's act and the
  receiver's reason for dropping the copy does not change it.  Applying
  PAWS first would have cleared flags on `tcp_reorder_ts` (a spurious
  resend delayed on the slow path can carry a TSval older than the newest
  in-order segment by the time it lands) — which is exactly the
  over-correction the plan named the file as the guard against.
- **Verified against the tree at Phase 0:** `tcp_wrap_ts` under defaults
  has decode digest `1b7cb603…` at commit `493b902` and now; under
  `no-hold` it differs only by Phase 2's late-reason rename.  The test pins
  the default digest.
- The plan's "`max_tsval`" as the clock for the far-side rule became
  `ts_recent`, since `max_tsval` was removed in Phase 2 and the receiver's
  own value is the right reference anyway.
- 393 tests become 413; the sixteen pins hold; neither rule fires on any of
  the six under either policy.
- **Found while answering why the hold is unrepresentable: the format
  cannot place a transport record more than 2 GiB past the origin at all.**
  The origin floor is a serial comparison, so a record 2³¹ past `isn + 1`
  is "below" it: python-zipline's writer refuses it with the handshake
  seen, and without the handshake the reader zero-widths every later record
  while `unplaceable` stays empty (the checker skips the floor without an
  `isn`; `record_ranges()` does not).  A `0.2.0` conversion of any capture
  with more than 2 GiB in one direction failed outright, and no fixture
  anywhere exceeds 1 MiB.  Filed as
  [zipline#146](https://github.com/adamkjonsson/zipline/issues/146) (offsets
  should unwrap along stored order — the ordering rule already keeps
  consecutive records within 2³¹),
  [zipline#147](https://github.com/adamkjonsson/zipline/issues/147) (a hole
  of 2³¹ or more between consecutive records has no representation; the
  `capture-gap` split is the conformant workaround) and
  [python-zipline#70](https://github.com/adamkjonsson/python-zipline/issues/70)
  (the checker/`ranges()` disagreement).  **A temporary shim ships in this
  release**, the first this project has carried: a direction is split into a
  new session every 2 GiB, ending for `offset-limit`, through the same
  `splits_before` hook — `_OFFSET_LIMIT`, `END_OFFSET_LIMIT`,
  `_Stream.origin`, `stats.offset_splits`, all marked `SHIM — zipline#146`
  and listed in `docs/internals/upstream-first.md`.  413 tests become 417.

## Phase 4 — #18: `favor-acked` — **DONE** (2026-09-16)

The design, since #18 said the design is the work.

### The policy

- [x] [`OVERLAP_POLICIES`](../src/zpfwire/options.py#L21) becomes
  `("favor-old", "favor-acked")`, and its docstring — which currently argues
  that one value is all a single-pass writer can honour — becomes the
  argument for why the second one *can* be: the hold is on runs, before the
  writer sees them. The CLI's `--overlap` follows through `choices=`.
- [x] `favor-acked` keeps, for any range that arrived more than once with
  **differing content**, the copy whose TSval the peer's first covering ACK
  echoed as TSecr. Where the copies agree byte-for-byte the choice is moot
  and nothing is held back on their account. Where the evidence is absent —
  no option, no reverse direction, no ACK before the bound — it **falls back
  to `favor-old`, and the fallback is counted** (`acked_fallback_bytes`),
  so the digest on the Decoder Descriptor never names a rule the file
  silently did not follow.
- [x] **No new option field.** The hold is bounded by the two bounds the
  policy already has: `flush_timeout` (capture seconds a closed run may wait
  for the ack that covers it) and `reorder_window` (bytes of closed runs a
  direction may hold). Adding a field would change `params_digest()` for
  every file, including `favor-old` ones — a `Breaking:` on the whole
  corpus for an option most files do not use. If either bound proves wrong
  for this purpose in practice, that is a `0.4.0` question with a real
  capture behind it.

### The mechanism

- [x] `_Run` gains `contributors: list[_Sent]` (already implied by Phase 1's
  `recent`) and `alternates: dict[tuple[int, int], _Alternate]` — for each
  sub-range that arrived again with different bytes, the losing copy's
  `(tsval, data, payload_offset)`. Only *differing* copies are kept, which
  bounds memory to genuine disagreements rather than to every repeat.
- [x] `_Run` gains `deferred: list[_Citation]` — the Undecoded citations a
  discard would have written immediately under `favor-old`. Under
  `favor-acked` the loser is not known until the ack, so the citation waits
  with the run and is written for whichever copy lost.
- [x] [`_flush`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py#L498) under `favor-acked` moves the
  closed run onto `_Stream.unacked: deque[_Run]` instead of writing it.
  Writing happens in `_settle(stream, ack, tsecr)`, called from
  [`packet()`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py#L136) for the *peer* stream on every
  packet that carries an ack: each unacked run whose end the ack covers
  (`_delta(ack, run_end) >= 0`) is decided — for each alternate whose TSval
  equals `tsecr` while the kept copy's does not, the alternate's bytes are
  spliced in, `acked_swaps` counted, and the deferred citation written
  against the copy that lost — and then written through `session.record()`
  in deque order, which preserves ascending `seq_start` per participant.
- [x] **The three hints are recomputed at settle time**, from the
  contributors as finally chosen: `ts` is the last contributor's time (a
  swapped-in resend moves it later, which is the specification's rule —
  that *is* the packet that completed the unit), `ts_first` the earliest,
  `ack` the serial maximum. Nothing in [`_append`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py#L298)'s
  rule changes; it moves.
- [x] `RETRANSMIT` is set whenever a resend contributed or competed,
  whichever copy won — the flag is the sender's act, and the sender resent.
- [x] **Bounds.** In `_maybe_commit_gap`'s spirit, `_settle_stale(stream,
  now)` runs on every packet: an unacked run older than `flush_timeout`, or
  the oldest run when the deque exceeds `reorder_window` bytes, is written
  as `favor-old` with `acked_fallback_bytes` counted. [`finish()`](../src/zpfwire/tcp.py#L163)
  settles everything left the same way, so session end never loses a run.
- [x] **Interactions, each re-argued and written down in the reassembler
  doc:** `coalesce=False` closes a run per segment and each is held (many
  small runs, same rule); `--sequenced` is unaffected because records still
  reach zpf in per-direction order and the linearisation is zpf's; the
  session `timeout` path runs `finish()`; a one-way capture fills the deque
  to `flush_timeout` and then falls back for every run — counted, and the
  summary says so.
- [x] **The caveat from the issue**, in the docstring: `TS.Recent` also
  updates from an old duplicate inside the delayed-ACK window (RFC 7323
  §4.3), so a copy landing there can steal the echo. Strong evidence, not
  proof; independent of capture point, which capture-order guessing is not.

### What Phase 4 execution changed

- **The evidence is per alternate, from the first ack covering *its*
  range — not the ack that releases the run.** The plan's `_settle(stream,

  acknowledged the echo names a later segment than the resend that filled
  the hole.  `_Alternate` now carries `decided`/`evidence`, stamped by the
  first ack covering it — on held *and open* runs — and `_write` reads them.
  All 5.
- **The generated corrupt-then-resend stream is not a valid test, and the
  plan's first test was dropped.** packeteer's corruption pass moves only
  the ack *answering* the corrupted segment behind the resend; the acks
  answering later segments — cumulative, and so covering the corrupted
  range — stay where they were, seventy packets before the resend.  A real
  receiver never acks bytes its checksum rejected.  Under `favor-acked` the
  generator's stream therefore settles every corrupted run before its
  resend arrives, on an ack that echoes neither copy.  The hand-built cases
  and `tcp_corrupt_ts.pcap` carry the test instead; the generator defect is
  [packeteer#163](https://github.com/adamkjonsson/packeteer/issues/163).

  the ack packet's own `_settle` runs before the stale sweep, so a bound
  checked only per packet never fired when the ack was the next thing in.
  The moment the hold can exceed `reorder_window` is when a run closes.
- **The hints moved from incremental fields on `_Run` to `_hints(run)`**,
  computed from `contributors` at write time, for both policies.  A
  swapped-in resend takes `ts` with it and the displaced copy stops
  counting towards `ts_first`; for `favor-old` the function returns what

## Decisions

### 1. Is #18 in `0.3.0`? — **Yes** (taken 2026-09-15)

The alternative — ship #17 alone and move #18 to `0.4.0` — was put and
declined. The argument for holding it was that no real capture shows a
byte-level difference between `favor-acked` and `favor-old` (every real
resend is byte-identical to its original), so the policy would ship
validated only against a generated corrupt-then-resend stream. The
argument that won: the *mechanism* is what the real captures validate —
the echo is real, 14 of 14 in `tcp_lossy_ts` and every fill in
`tcp_gap_ts` — and the byte difference is exactly the case the checksum
policy exists for, which the generator has produced since `0.12.0`. The
design is now concrete enough to phase (Phase 4), and the milestone was set
with both.

*Overtaken the same day:* [packeteer#160](https://github.com/adamkjonsson/packeteer/issues/160)
delivered `tcp_corrupt_ts.pcap` within hours of being filed, so the premise
of the argument for holding — no real byte difference — no longer holds
either. The decision stands on firmer ground than it was taken on.

### 2. How much of (4)? — **All of it** (taken 2026-09-15)

PAWS rejection and the wrap rule both land, with the real captures as the
proof that neither fires where a real receiver did not, and hand-built
frames as the proof that each fires where it should. "Plumb `ts_recent`
and count what would have been rejected" was the fallback and was not
needed: the `ts_recent` update rule that makes PAWS safe on real traffic
(in-order arrivals only) is the same one Phase 1 needs anyway.

### 3. No new option field for the hold (taken 2026-09-15)

`favor-acked` is bounded by `flush_timeout` and `reorder_window` rather
than by a field of its own, because `params_digest()` hashes every field
and a new one would move the Decoder Descriptor on every file, `favor-old`
included. A dedicated bound can be argued for in `0.4.0` from a capture
that needed one.

### 4. What the duplicate rule does *not* use (taken 2026-09-15)

Not capture-time distance (0.4 µs against 40 ms in the corpus, but any
threshold is a guess a different capture point invalidates), and not
payload content (a resend has the same bytes; corruption is the checksum
policy's). Same TSval, nothing from the peer in between, both sides
carrying the option — and the spec's SHOULD for everything else.
